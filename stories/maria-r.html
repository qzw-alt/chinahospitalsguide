Maria R. is an international patient diagnosed with two rare vascular compression syndromes. She has now been connected directly with Tangdu Hospital in Xi'an, where a three-department multidisciplinary team (MDT) has reviewed her case. As her case advances, this page is updated.

The Diagnosis: Two Rare Vascular Compression Syndromes

Maria has been diagnosed with two separate anatomical vascular compressions — both uncommon, and both affecting the venous (return) side of her circulation:

Nutcracker Syndrome occurs when the left renal vein is compressed between the abdominal aorta (behind it) and the superior mesenteric artery (in front of it) — like a nut between the arms of a nutcracker. The result is impaired blood flow out of the left kidney, which can cause blood in the urine, left-sided flank pain, and pelvic congestion. It is most often seen in younger, slimmer patients.

May-Thurner Syndrome (iliac vein compression syndrome) occurs when the left common iliac vein is squeezed against the spine by the overlying right common iliac artery. This raises pressure in the left leg and sharply increases the risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), chronic leg swelling, and venous insufficiency.

Having both conditions in one patient is unusual. It means the venous circulation needs to be assessed as a whole — which is exactly why this case requires a hospital with a strong vascular and interventional platform rather than a quick single-specialist opinion.

Why Tangdu Hospital, Xi'an

Tangdu Hospital (唐都医院) is one of Xi'an's two leading military general hospitals, affiliated with the Air Force Medical University (formerly the Fourth Military Medical University, FMMU). It consistently ranks among China's top 20 hospitals and is known for interventional cardiology, oncology, and trauma care — a vascular and interventional platform well suited to vascular compression cases.

Rather than guessing which department fits, we are submitting Maria's records to Tangdu's dedicated international medical office, which handles remote review for foreign patients: the hospital's specialists review the imaging and reports first, then return a cost estimate and a proposed timeline. That review is the step we are waiting on.
