Cervical cancer is one of the few cancers where the cure depends on a single, early fact: how far the disease has spread when it is found. Stage it early and the treatment is often one surgery, with an excellent chance of leaving you cancer-free. Stage it a little later and the answer shifts to a roughly six-week course of radiation, built around a technique that is easy to do poorly and unavailable in a surprising number of countries. This article walks through that fork so you can see where you are on it, what each path involves, and what it costs in China.

Our dedicated [cervical cancer page](/conditions/cervical-cancer/) collects the full pathway, hospitals and costs in one place; this post explains the decision logic behind it.

## 1. Staging Is the First Question

Before any treatment is chosen, a team stages the disease, usually on the FIGO system, which describes how far the tumor has reached. The practical divisions for treatment are three:

- **Early stage (roughly FIGO I to IIA).** The tumor is confined to the cervix or has reached only the top of the vagina. This is where surgery is usually the first and only treatment.
- **Locally advanced (roughly FIGO IIB to IVA).** The tumor has spread into nearby tissue, the lower vagina, or the pelvic wall. Surgery is generally not the first move; the standard is concurrent chemoradiation.
- **Metastatic or recurrent (FIGO IVB).** The disease has spread beyond the pelvis. Treatment shifts to systemic therapy rather than a curative local procedure.

Staging in China typically means a pelvic examination and biopsy, followed by pelvic MRI and PET-CT to measure the tumor and check lymph nodes. At a Grade 3A cancer center this workup can usually be arranged within a few days, so the decision path starts quickly.

## 2. The Treatment Path by Stage

**Early stage: surgery.** For small, early tumors the standard is a radical hysterectomy with lymph-node assessment, which removes the cervix, uterus and surrounding tissue in one operation. Where the tumor is small enough, a fertility-preserving alternative exists: a radical trachelectomy removes the cervix but keeps the uterus, so pregnancy may remain possible. Whether that option fits depends on tumor size and stage, and a gynecologic oncology team decides case by case.

**Locally advanced: chemoradiation plus brachytherapy.** This is the standard when the disease has reached nearby tissue. It combines external-beam radiation, weekly cisplatin, and internal radiation known as brachytherapy, where a source is placed directly against the tumor, often guided by MRI or CT. The full course runs about five to six weeks. The brachytherapy component matters more than it sounds: it is the part of cervical cancer radiation most strongly tied to cure, yet it needs dedicated equipment and specialist teams and is not offered everywhere.

**Metastatic or recurrent: systemic therapy.** When the disease is beyond the pelvis, the goal is control rather than cure, using chemotherapy, the targeted agent bevacizumab, or immunotherapy for tumors that express PD-L1. Treatment is planned cycle by cycle.

The insight worth holding onto: **most cervical cancer is caught at a stage where a single, well-executed treatment is genuinely curative.** The question is not whether treatment exists, but whether you get the right one for your stage.

## 3. What Each Stage Costs

Reference prices for international patients, with US figures shown for comparison. These are estimates. The final quote comes from the hospital after it reviews your records and imaging.

| Treatment | Reference cost (China) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Staging workup (pelvic MRI + PET-CT) | $1,200–$2,200 | before a plan is set |
| Radical hysterectomy (early stage) | $6,000–$12,000 | vs $30,000+ in the US |
| Chemoradiation + brachytherapy (full course) | $12,000–$20,000 | standard for locally advanced disease |
| Systemic therapy (chemo / bevacizumab / immunotherapy) | $1,000–$3,000 | per cycle; varies by regimen |

The numbers separate neatly. Early-stage surgery is the least expensive path, and the locally advanced radiation course is a larger but still bounded figure that covers roughly six weeks of treatment. Either way, the gap against Western self-pay rates is wide, which is a large part of why international patients consider China for this diagnosis.

## 4. Where Each Stage Is Best Handled

China's national cancer centers deliver the full range, but a few stand out for cervical cancer specifically.

- **Cancer Hospital, CAMS** (Beijing) — China's number-one oncology center, with high-volume gynecologic oncology surgery and radiotherapy. See [CAMS](/hospitals/cams-cancer-hospital/).
- **Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center** (Guangzhou) — a top-three national cancer hospital and a leading southern center for both surgery and radiation. See [Sun Yat-sen](/hospitals/sun-yat-sen-cancer-hospital/).
- **Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center** (Shanghai) — a top-three oncology center with one of the country's strongest gynecologic oncology departments. See [Fudan Cancer Center](/hospitals/fudan-university-shanghai-cancer-center/).
- **Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute & Hospital** (Tianjin) — a leading northern cancer center with strong radiotherapy. See [Tianjin](/hospitals/tianjin-medical-university-cancer/).
- **Fudan University Women's Hospital (Red House)** (Shanghai) — China's number-two obstetrics and gynecology hospital, a dedicated women's center. See [Fudan Women's](/hospitals/fudan-womens-hospital/).

The full selection logic, including why no single center tops every case, is on our [methodology page](/how-we-choose-hospitals.html).

## 5. What This Does Not Promise

Three honest limits, because a curable cancer invites overstatement:

1. **A plan is not a guarantee.** Cervical cancer is highly treatable when caught early, but no one can promise an individual outcome. The numbers cited here are starting points, not a prediction for your case.
2. **The prices are estimates.** A final quote depends on your stage, the surgical approach, and how long the radiation course runs. Get a written quote before you commit.
3. **Fertility preservation is not always possible.** A trachelectomy fits only selected small tumors. Many patients will still need a hysterectomy, and that is a decision only a gynecologic oncology team can make with your imaging in hand.

The honest frame is: **China's advantage here is volume and access to brachytherapy**, delivered as a routine part of a planned course rather than a waiting-list lottery, at a fraction of Western self-pay cost.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions

### Which hospital is best for cervical cancer in China?
The Cancer Hospital of CAMS (Beijing) is China's number-one oncology center, and Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (Guangzhou) and Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center are top-three national cancer hospitals. All three provide external-beam radiation and image-guided brachytherapy as a planned course. Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute is a leading northern center, and Fudan Women's Hospital (Shanghai) is a dedicated gynecology center. A case review confirms which of these fits your stage and timing.

### How much does cervical cancer treatment cost in China?
Reference ranges: staging workup $1,200–$2,200, radical hysterectomy $6,000–$12,000 for early-stage disease, and a full course of concurrent chemoradiation with brachytherapy $12,000–$20,000. Systemic therapy runs $1,000–$3,000 per cycle depending on the regimen. Your final quote depends on your stage and the plan chosen.

### How quickly can cervical cancer treatment start in China?
Records can be reviewed before you travel, and treatment is usually scheduled within two to four weeks once a plan is agreed. For locally advanced disease the chemoradiation course itself runs about five to six weeks. Timing is confirmed for your case after the records are assessed.

### Can I preserve fertility while treating cervical cancer in China?
In selected early-stage cases, yes. A radical trachelectomy removes the cervix while keeping the uterus, offering a fertility-preserving alternative to hysterectomy for certain small tumors. Whether you qualify depends on tumor size and stage, and a gynecologic oncology team must assess it.

### What is brachytherapy and why does it matter?
Brachytherapy is internal radiation placed directly against the tumor, often guided by MRI or CT, and it is the part of cervical cancer radiation most strongly tied to cure. Because it needs dedicated equipment and specialist teams, it is not available everywhere. China's national cancer centers deliver it as a routine part of the chemoradiation course.
