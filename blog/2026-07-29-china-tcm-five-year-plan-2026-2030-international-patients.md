## China's State Council Just Set TCM Targets for the Next Five Years

On 23 July 2026, China's State Council approved a new five-year plan for traditional Chinese medicine that runs through 2030. The plan was jointly released by the **National Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine** and the **National Development and Reform Commission**, and it does something unusual for a TCM policy document — it commits to specific disease-level clinical breakthroughs on the same timeline as pharmaceutical research targets.

The headline goal: by 2030, China aims to establish a TCM service system that covers the entire population across the full life cycle, with breakthroughs in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, cancer, kidney disorders, and infectious diseases — the same four disease categories that dominate the country's Western-medicine research agenda.

This matters for international patients because the plan is not abstract. It builds on what is already in place at every Chinese tertiary hospital today.

> Source: China Daily / Xinhua, "China to boost traditional Chinese medicine inheritance, innovation, capacity," published 23 July 2026 ([chinadaily.com.cn](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202607/23/WS6a6183bfa310986e2b466e61.html))

Before the 2030 targets, the plan benchmarks against the 14th Five-Year Plan period (2021-2025). The data is concrete:

By the end of 2025, **nearly all county-level regions had established government-run TCM medical institutions** — meaning a foreign patient travelling almost anywhere in China can reach a public TCM hospital within a 1-2 hour drive.

</div>

The 96.26% figure is the most important one for medical tourists. It means when a foreign patient is referred to a Western tertiary hospital in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, or Hangzhou, the TCM department is already there as an institutional peer — not an alternative-medicine clinic tucked into a corner.

For comparison: in most US hospitals, an "integrative medicine" or "acupuncture" service, if it exists, is a separate clinic with separate billing and no formal connection to the oncology or cardiology department. In China, the TCM physician often sits in the same multidisciplinary team meeting.

The plan also names two clinical capacity gaps that international patients frequently hit: **geriatric care** and **pediatric care**. TCM hospitals are directed to set up mental health and sleep clinics, and to strengthen their capacity for elderly and pediatric patients. This is a direct response to China's aging population and low birth rate — but it also creates a clear access pathway for foreign families looking for non-pharmaceutical sleep and behavioral-health support.

## The "High-Quality Integration" Clause — and Why It Matters for Foreign Cancer Patients

The most concrete clinical commitment in the 2026-30 plan is the **"high-quality integration of TCM and Western medicine"** language. This is not new rhetoric — China has had integrated medicine for decades — but the plan upgrades it from a slogan to a research and capacity-building mandate.

For an international cancer patient, this translates to a specific, repeatable experience at any major Chinese cancer center:

<div class="integration-note">

**What an integrative oncology consult looks like at Peking University Cancer Hospital, Fudan Shanghai Cancer Center, or Ruijin Hospital:**

- TCM physician and Western oncologist see the patient in the same visit
- Herbal prescriptions are written to manage chemo-induced nausea, fatigue, and neutropenia — these are dispensed in the hospital pharmacy alongside Western drugs
- Acupuncture for chemo-induced peripheral neuropathy is available as an in-house referral
- Baduanjin qigong and other TCM rehabilitation exercises are prescribed as part of the discharge plan
- All herbal prescriptions are checked against the patient's chemo regimen by a clinical pharmacist trained in herb-drug interactions

</div>

None of this is experimental. It is the standard of care inside China's tertiary cancer centers. The 2026-30 plan commits the government to keep funding this infrastructure through 2030, which for foreign patients means it will not quietly disappear when budgets tighten.

## What the Plan Says About TCM Going Global — and What That Means for Access

The plan's international language is short but specific: China will "further expand opening up in the TCM sector, encourage the industry to play a more active role in global health governance, and deepen international exchanges and mutual learning."

In practice, the 2025-2026 data already shows this happening:

- **Germany:** Anhui Jiren Pharmaceutical's Shufeng Jiedu granules have been on the shelves of the Bahnhof-Apotheke pharmacy chain for over six years — nearly 200,000 bottles sold at €39.90 each.
- **Russia:** Tianjin Da Ren Tang's Suxiao Jiuxin Pills have been a registered prescription heart-attack medication since 1997.
- **Hainan:** The Sanya Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine has built dedicated foreign-client pathways, with Russian and Gulf-state patients making up the bulk of inbound TCM-only stays.

For an international patient considering a TCM-only stay in China, the practical question is not whether the government supports TCM — that question has been settled for decades. The question is which hospital can serve a foreign patient in English or Bahasa Indonesia or Vietnamese, with consistent herbal quality, and with a Western-trained physician on call if anything goes wrong.

For most foreign patients, that narrows the list to:

| Hospital | Best for | Foreign-patient infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanya Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine** (Hainan) | TCM-only stays, wellness + chronic disease | Visa-free access for most Asian/Middle East passports; English-speaking international department |
| **Dongzhimen Hospital** (Beijing, BUCM affiliate) | Chronic disease, post-stroke recovery, oncology support | International department; English/Mandarin |
| **Shanghai Yueyang Hospital** (Integrated TCM-Western) | Pain, rheumatology, post-stroke | International patient services; English available |
| **Guang'anmen Hospital** (CACMS, Beijing) | Cardiovascular, oncology support | International department; English available |
| **First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of TCM** | Fertility, gynecology, pain | Cantonese/English; strong foreign-patient pathways |

For patients comparing cost across the region, a 10-14 day TCM course in China runs **roughly 60-75% less than comparable TCM programs in Singapore, Korea, or the United States** — and Singapore has very limited TCM capacity outside of traditional Chinese medicine practitioners without hospital integration. China's lead is not just price. It is the institutional integration of TCM with Western medicine inside the same hospital system, which very few countries outside China, Korea, and parts of Germany can match.

## What to Watch Over the Next 12-18 Months

The plan does not name specific delivery dates, but several concrete signals will show whether it is on track:

1. **Pediatric and geriatric TCM departments opening at provincial cancer and cardiology centers** — these are the most under-built areas today, and foreign families with elderly parents or children with chronic conditions should watch for them.
2. **Mental health and sleep clinics inside TCM hospitals** — the plan explicitly directs TCM hospitals to build these. For foreign patients who want non-benzodiazepine sleep support or anxiety management, this is the most concrete new capacity.
3. **International department capacity at Tier-2 city TCM hospitals** — today, most foreign patients cluster at the four or five best-known hospitals. If the plan expands international capacity to provincial capitals, it will materially change what foreign patients can access.
4. **The integration of TCM into oncology clinical trial protocols** — the plan calls for "clarifying the mechanisms" of TCM in disease prevention and treatment. This means more published RCTs, which in turn will make TCM support easier for foreign oncologists to recommend.

For patients planning a medical trip in 2026 or 2027, none of these are abstract. They will show up as concrete new service lines at the hospitals where they are already being seen.
