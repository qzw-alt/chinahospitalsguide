Planning a hospital visit in China from another country tends to stall on two questions that a search engine cannot answer well. The first is "which hospital is actually good?" The second is "what will this actually cost?" Both are easy to ask and very hard to resolve from abroad, where you cannot walk a corridor, meet a doctor, or read a Chinese price list. This site exists to close that gap. Here is how it works, and what we built in 2026 to make it easier.

## 1. The First Wall: "Which Hospital Is Actually Good?"

When you search for the best hospital in China for your condition, what you mostly get back is marketing. Ranking pages are assembled for search traffic, not for your diagnosis, and a hospital's own website will never tell you it is the wrong fit. From another country, the usual vetting tools — reputation, referrals, a first-hand visit — are out of reach.

We approach this the way a local would. Our [hospital directory](/hospitals.html) holds **52 hospital pages**, each built around verifiable facts rather than slogans: JCI accreditation or China's Grade 3A rating, the specialties the hospital is known for, and whether it runs an international department with English-speaking staff and a track record for overseas patients. The full selection logic is written out on our [methodology page](/how-we-choose-hospitals.html), so you can see the criteria instead of taking our word for it.

The deeper answer is that a shortlist is only as good as the follow-up. A hospital that looks excellent on paper may not accept your specific case, or may not answer at all. That is why our paid tiers do the part you cannot do from abroad: actually contacting the right department and getting a response.

## 2. The Second Wall: "What Will It Actually Cost?"

Chinese public hospitals do not publish price lists aimed at international patients, and international wards are private-pay, which means the number you find online is often stale or wrong. You can spend days chasing a figure that a hospital may still revise once it sees your records.

We built a [free cost calculator](/calculator.html) to give you a defensible starting point in about a minute. It covers **nine common procedures** — knee and hip replacement, heart bypass and valve surgery, cancer treatment, spine surgery, IVF, cataract, and LASIK — and adjusts by **city** (from Beijing's top-tier centers to Chengdu's value options) and **hospital tier** (a Grade 3A center, a JCI international department, or a regional value hospital). The result shows a China estimate next to a US and UK reference and your percentage savings.

The calculator is an estimate, not a quote, and it says so. A real number only comes from a hospital reviewing your records — which is exactly what the paid service arranges. This is the honest order of operations: estimate first for free, then verify with the hospital before you commit.

## 3. How the Service Works

We are not a directory and not a booking agent taking a cut from hospitals. We are a **Chinese-speaking intermediary** who knows which department answers the phone. That distinction shapes everything, starting with how we are paid: **we charge patients a flat fee and take no commission from hospitals**, so the hospital we recommend is the one that fits your case, not the one that pays us.

The service runs in three tiers, each building on the one before it, and you can upgrade at any time and only pay the difference:

- **$49 — Hospital Shortlist.** You tell us your condition and timeline; we return two to three hospitals matched to your case, with cost comparison across cities and travel guidance. Information only, no hospital contact.
- **$149 — Hospital Verification.** We send your case summary in Chinese to two or three hospitals, translate their responses into English, and connect you directly with the best fit. Delivered in four to seven business days.
- **$399 — Full Journey Management.** Everything in the $149 tier, plus medical record translation, a visa invitation letter, appointment booking, airport pickup, and coordination through discharge and follow-up.

The full breakdown, including refund terms, is on the [pricing page](/pricing.html). The free starting point — browsing hospitals, running the calculator, reading the guides — is genuinely free.

## 4. What We Added in 2026

The site has moved well beyond a static list this year. The pieces that matter most if you are planning a trip now:

- **The cost calculator**, described above, turns an opaque process into a number you can share with your family before you spend anything.
- **52 hospital detail pages** replace a thin directory with structured, fact-based profiles you can compare side by side.
- **Country landing pages** in English, Russian, Arabic, and Indonesian, plus a Singapore page, speak to patients in their own language about the routes and costs that apply to them.
- **A dedicated [heart-failure entry point](/heart-failure.html)** covers a condition where China's treatment options, including newer device and surgical routes, differ enough from Western defaults to matter.
- **A [transit visa guide](/visa-guide.html)** and an [imaging fast-track](/medical-imaging.html) — an MRI or CT booked within a day or two — remove the two most common logistical blockers.

None of these replace a doctor. They exist to get you to the right one with less guesswork.

## 5. What This Does Not Claim

Three honest limits, because "we will match you to a hospital" invites overstatement:

1. **We do not treat, and we do not guarantee outcomes.** We connect patients to hospitals; the clinical decisions and results belong to the doctors. No intermediary can promise a cure, and you should be wary of any that do.
2. **An estimate is not a quote.** The calculator and the published ranges are starting points. The final number depends on your records, the materials, and the hospital's assessment — and it can move.
3. **China is not the right answer for everyone.** Many conditions are handled perfectly well at home. The service earns its keep when you need a therapy only approved in China, when your home wait time is dangerously long, or when the equivalent treatment is out of reach on price.

The honest frame is not "China is better." It is "for a specific set of patients, China is a serious option, and you should be able to evaluate it without flying blind."
