On August 8, 2026, surgeons at Ghurki Trust Teaching Hospital in Lahore removed a bone tumor from a 26-year-old patient's left hip socket and rebuilt the joint with a custom 3D-printed partial pelvic implant made in China. The patient had a rare inherited bone condition and had developed chondrosarcoma, a form of bone cancer, in the socket of the left hip. Removing the tumor meant removing bone — and that is exactly the situation where off-the-shelf implants fail and patient-specific manufacturing takes over.

The implant came from Farshine Medical, a Chinese firm that chose Pakistan as the first market in its overseas expansion. The operation was the hospital's first use of patient-specific instruments and 3D printing in orthopedic cancer surgery, and it was led by Prof Amer Aziz with Ali Ijaz Ahmad, the clinical lead for sarcoma services, on the team. What happened in Lahore is worth understanding for any patient facing complex bone reconstruction, because the same technology — and the same manufacturers — sit at the end of a direct flight in China.

## The Case: Tumor Out, Custom Implant In

The surgeons removed the cancer-affected part of the pelvis while preserving the patient's leg, then fitted the custom-designed 3D-printed partial pelvic implant and performed a total hip replacement in the same operation. Conventional reconstruction of the pelvis is notoriously difficult: the bone is oddly shaped, load-bearing and surrounded by nerves and vessels that cannot be sacrificed. A one-size-fits-all implant rarely fits, which is why many centers still respond to pelvic tumors with amputation or hemipelvectomy — the loss of the entire leg.

Before the operation, the team rehearsed. Using a printed model of the tumor and patient-specific surgical guides, they planned where to cut the bone, how the implant would sit and how the hip would be reconstructed. Ali Ijaz Ahmad described the rehearsal as a "mock surgery" — a critical step that let the team verify bone cuts, guide positioning and implant fit before anyone entered the operating room. That rehearsal is the difference between a two-hour routine replacement and an eight-hour reconstruction with guesswork in the middle.

## What a Chinese 3D-Printed Implant Is

A patient-specific implant starts with a CT scan of the patient's own anatomy. Engineers design the replacement to match the exact bone removed — including the screw paths, joint surfaces and the way the implant will share load with the remaining pelvis — and a 3D printer builds it layer by layer in titanium alloy or PEEK. The same digital workflow produces the surgical guides that tell the surgeon precisely where to cut. Nothing is stock; everything is designed for one body.

China has turned this workflow into an industry. The National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) has cleared a growing list of 3D-printed implant products, and major orthopedic centers — Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, Beijing Jishuitan Hospital, Third Xiangya Hospital in Changsha — have published extensive experience with custom implants for bone tumors, trauma and joint revision. Our [full guide to 3D-printed implants in China](/blog/3d-printed-implants-china.html) covers the products, the hospitals and the approval pathway in detail.

The Lahore case shows the same supply chain working at export. Farshine Medical told China Economic Net that it picked Pakistan as its first overseas market because of close bilateral ties, geographic proximity and a favorable policy environment — and that it plans to work with more Pakistani hospitals and enter other medical fields. For the patient, the nationality of the manufacturer matters less than the capability: the design-and-print loop that rebuilt this pelvis is the same one available inside China, where the manufacturers, the regulators and the surgical teams all sit in one country.

## Why China Leads Custom Implant Manufacturing

Three structural advantages explain the lead. First, volume: Chinese hospitals treat a very large number of complex orthopedic and bone-tumor cases per year, so the design teams at implant manufacturers see more anatomical variation in a month than most markets see in a year. Second, the regulatory pathway: NMPA's review of 3D-printed and patient-specific devices is now well-trodden, which shortens the distance from a hospital's CT scan to an approved, cleared implant. Third, cost of iteration: when a design needs adjustment — a different screw angle, a smaller flange — the digital workflow makes the change cheap and fast, and the manufacturing base keeps it that way.

The result is a capability tier that most markets cannot match locally. In Southeast Asia, Central Asia and the Middle East, complex pelvic, jaw, rib and long-bone reconstructions are frequently referred out of country precisely because the implant supply chain does not exist at home. China sits a short flight away from all of them, with the full stack — design, print, approval, surgery, rehabilitation — in one place. For context on the wider set of procedures where China leads, see our [guide to China's unique medical procedures](/blog/china-unique-medical-procedures-guide.html).

## China-Pakistan Medical Cooperation Is Moving Fast

The Lahore operation is one data point in a fast-deepening medical relationship. In November 2025, the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences in Islamabad performed the capital's first robotic adrenalectomy using the Chinese-developed Toumai surgical robot. Pakistan also leads the region in demand: the International Agency for Research on Cancer estimates roughly 190,300 new cancer cases and 119,700 cancer deaths in Pakistan in 2024, according to GLOBOCAN figures published in July 2026 — [the source data is public at IARC](https://gco.iarc.who.int/en/). Bone and soft-tissue sarcomas are a small share of that burden, but they are among the hardest to treat, and they disproportionately hit younger patients — exactly the demographic that shows up in operating rooms like Ghurki Trust's.

For Pakistani patients, the choice is not just "China versus home." It is China versus the Gulf, Singapore or Turkey for complex oncology surgery. On cost, custom-implant reconstruction in China undercuts Singapore and Gulf pricing by a wide margin; on capability, the 3D-printed implant supply chain is a genuine differentiator that most referral destinations cannot offer at any price. Pakistani patients have been coming to China for years — most visibly for [CAR-T cell therapy](/blog/car-t-therapy-china-2026.html), where Chinese hospitals treat more international patients than any other country — and custom orthopedic surgery is now part of the same corridor.

## What This Means for International Patients

If you or a family member faces a bone tumor, a failed joint replacement with severe bone loss, or a complex fracture that standard implants cannot handle, the practical question is whether a custom implant changes the outcome. For pelvic tumors, jaw reconstruction, rib resection and long-segment bone loss, the answer is often yes — and China is one of the few places where the entire chain works under one roof.

Chinese hospitals' international departments handle the full path: remote review of imaging, CT-based implant design, a written treatment plan with itemized costs, visa letters, airport pickup and translation through discharge. Costs are quoted per case because the implant is designed for one patient, but the overall package — implant, surgery, hospital stay — typically lands well below equivalent quotes in Singapore, Thailand or Western centers. [Spinal and orthopedic surgery pricing in China](/blog/spine-surgery-cost-china.html) follows the same pattern: world-standard technique, a fraction of the regional price.

One piece of the package international patients often do not expect is the rehabilitation layer. Chinese orthopedic and oncology centers routinely combine Western surgical care with [integrated TCM-western supportive treatment](/blog/2026-08-08-boao-lecheng-600-first-in-china-therapies-tcm-wellness/): acupuncture for post-operative pain, herbal formulas prescribed to support bone healing and reduce swelling, and tuina or moxibustion once the wound allows. This is not alternative therapy standing in for surgery — it runs alongside it, and for many patients it shortens the recovery window and cuts the painkiller load. The [TCM integration in Chinese hospitals](/blog/2026-07-29-china-tcm-five-year-plan-2026-2030-international-patients/) is now a standard, documented part of the care pathway rather than an add-on you have to request.

## How to Plan Custom Implant Surgery in China

1. **Get the imaging done at home.** A CT (and MRI, where relevant) of the affected bone is the starting point for both the surgical plan and the implant design. Chinese hospitals review scans remotely before you book anything.
2. **Pick the hospital by disease type.** For bone tumors and complex reconstruction, orthopedic oncology departments at Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, Beijing Jishuitan and similar centers are the usual starting points; their international departments respond to English inquiries directly.
3. **Ask for a written estimate.** The quote should itemize the implant design and printing, surgery, hospital stay and rehabilitation. Prices are per case, so a written quotation is the only number that matters.
4. **Plan the timeline.** Implant design and printing add roughly two to four weeks before surgery. Budget for that window when planning travel.
5. **Expect the integrated package.** Post-operative care typically includes both Western rehab protocols and TCM support — acupuncture, herbal medicine and tuina — which is part of why Chinese centers recover patients the way they do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a 3D-printed pelvic implant?**
A patient-specific bone replacement designed from CT scans and manufactured layer by layer, usually in titanium alloy or PEEK. For pelvic bone tumors, the implant matches the exact bone removed, which lets surgeons preserve the leg and reconstruct the hip in one operation — the approach used in Lahore.

**Which Chinese hospitals perform custom 3D-printed implant surgery?**
Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, Beijing Jishuitan Hospital and Third Xiangya Hospital in Changsha are among the centers with long-running 3D-printing programs, and NMPA has cleared multiple 3D-printed implant products. Their international departments assess overseas patients case by case.

**How much does custom implant surgery cost in China?**
Quoted per case, because the implant is designed for one patient's anatomy. Packages covering implant, surgery and hospital stay generally run well below equivalent quotes in Singapore, Thailand or Western centers, and written itemized estimates come before any commitment.

**Can international patients access this surgery in China?**
Yes. International patient services handle remote imaging review, implant design, visa letters, translation and coordination. Referral letters and scans can be reviewed before travel, and the treatment plan is fixed in writing in advance.
