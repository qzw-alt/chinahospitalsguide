## The Zone That Imports the Future, and Now Exports Wellness

On August 7, 2026, Hainan's Boao Lecheng International Medical Tourism Pilot Zone hosted a press tour that doubled as a status report. The headline numbers: **more than 600 drugs, medical devices and special food products are now available at Lecheng for the first time in China**, a Korean aesthetic hospital has opened inside the zone, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) wellness programs are running at the Boao Yiling Life-Care Center — all under the "medical care + tourism" model the zone has built on the opening-up dividends of the Hainan Free Trade Port. [The full release was carried by PR Newswire](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/08/07/tmt-newswire/pr-newswire/check-out-hainans-pioneering-medical-hub/2400902).

For an international patient, Lecheng is the single most important address in Chinese medical tourism that most people outside Asia have never heard of. It is a government-designated pilot zone where hospitals can import treatments that are approved abroad but not yet on the mainland market — meaning the newest drugs, devices and therapies reach patients in China months or years before they would otherwise be available, and foreign patients can use them in the same zone.

## What the 600+ Approvals Mean in Practice

The "first in China" label is the whole point of the zone. Mainland drug approval normally requires domestic clinical trials and a full NMPA review, a process measured in years. Lecheng's pilot policy short-circuits that for products with urgent clinical need: if a drug or device is already approved in its country of origin and shows clear benefit, a hospital inside the zone can import and use it for eligible patients. The 600-plus count covers drugs, medical devices and special food products — oncology drugs, cardiology devices, cell and gene therapies, and specialty nutrition among them.

The zone also hosts the permanent International Innovative Drugs and Medical Devices Exhibition, which doubles as a cultural-tourism platform. For patients, the exhibition matters because it turns the zone into a place where the pipeline of new therapies is visible — you can see, in one location, what the next wave of global medicine looks like before it reaches most markets.

## A Korean Aesthetic Hospital Opens Inside the Zone

The newest institutional arrival is South Korea's **JK Medical Aesthetic & Plastic Surgery Hospital**, whose settlement the release describes as laying a solid foundation for China-Korea cooperation in medical and wellness tourism. That single move says more about where the zone is heading than any announcement: a Korean operator — from the country that has dominated Asia's aesthetic-surgery market for two decades — chose to open inside China's pilot zone rather than only receiving Chinese patients in Seoul.

That is the price-and-access signal in one decision. Korea's aesthetic market is expensive and saturated; China's is cheaper for comparable procedures, and Lecheng adds the policy layer that lets international patients combine treatment with a stay in a free-trade-port tourist destination. For patients from Southeast Asia, Russia and Central Asia, and the Middle East — the site's core readership — the calculation is straightforward: Korean-standard aesthetic care, Chinese pricing, and a visa-friendly island setting.

## TCM at the Zone: The Boao Yiling Life-Care Center

The wellness side of the zone is where traditional Chinese medicine gets a modern institutional home. At the **Boao Yiling Life-Care Center**, visitors can take part in traditional Chinese wellness therapies including **TCM bone-setting (中医正骨)** — the manual therapy school of Chinese medicine used for musculoskeletal conditions — and **calligraphy-based health preservation**, a meditative practice that uses brush writing as a form of focused relaxation. The center's wellness communities operate on a model the tour was shown directly: traditional wellness wisdom combined with modern scientific support.

This is the same integrated approach Chinese hospitals apply to clinical care. At major Chinese treatment centers, integrated Chinese-Western medicine (中西医结合) runs alongside oncology protocols, with acupuncture for nausea and chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, herbal formulas for fatigue and appetite loss, and tuina or moxibustion for pain and recovery — supportive care layered on top of the primary regimen. Lecheng extends that philosophy into the wellness and recovery phase, which is exactly the segment medical tourists increasingly want after treatment: somewhere to recover, not just somewhere to get treated. ## The Medical-Tourism Model: Treatment + Recovery in One Place

The zone's structure is the differentiator. Lecheng connects its special-medical-policy strengths with surrounding tourism infrastructure — Boao Golf Course and Dongyu Island Resort are named in the release — to deepen the integration of medical, wellness and tourism sectors. The result is a destination where a patient can complete an innovative treatment at a zone hospital, then move into a TCM wellness program or a coastal resort stay without leaving the pilot area.

That combination is rare anywhere in the world. Singapore has the medical infrastructure but not the wellness-and-resort layer at this scale; South Korea has the aesthetic expertise but not a policy zone built for importing first-in-China therapies; Bangkok and Kuala Lumpur have the medical tourism volume but not a dedicated pilot framework like this one. The zone's own framing — a "new benchmark for overseas tourists seeking premium wellness experiences" — is promotional, but the underlying structure is real: [China's medical tourism proposition](https://chinahospitalsguide.com/blog/why-medical-tourists-choose-china-over-thailand.html) rests on price, technology access and scale, and Lecheng adds the policy fast-track that other Asian hubs cannot replicate.

## How International Patients Access Lecheng Today

The access pathway for a foreign patient is the same one used at Chinese treatment centers generally, with the zone's hospitals handling the specifics:

1. **Contact a zone hospital's international department** — hospitals inside Lecheng have international patient teams that respond in English and coordinate the full pathway.
2. **Submit records for review** — the hospital's specialists assess whether the needed therapy is available in the zone and whether the patient qualifies under the pilot criteria.
3. **Get an itemized cost estimate before travel** — treatment plans are priced line by line, so there are no surprise bills on arrival.
4. **Use Hainan's streamlined visa procedures** — medical tourists get visa facilitation under the free trade port framework, and the island's visa-free entry rules cover a wide list of countries.
5. **Combine treatment with recovery** — the wellness layer (TCM programs, resorts, golf) is bookable alongside the medical stay.

For patients needing innovative oncology or cell therapy, the zone complements the commercial treatments available at mainland centers — including approved CAR-T products, which China already prices at a fraction of Western levels. ## What to Watch Next

Three signals worth tracking over the next 12 months: the full opening of JK Medical's Lecheng facility and the pricing it publishes (that will set the benchmark for Korean-standard aesthetic care in China); the next batch of first-in-China approvals, since the 600-plus count has been climbing steadily as the exhibition attracts more manufacturers; and how many of the zone's TCM wellness programs begin accepting international patients as formal medical-tourism products with published prices, rather than as tour experiences.
